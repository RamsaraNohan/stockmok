Reconciled, not redesigned
Every 390 px screen already drawn in Gates 5 to 10 was checked against this table rather than re-drawn. Where it already obeyed, it stands as evidence; the four boards that follow add only the flows that had no small-screen frame yet.
The phone is not a cut-down desktop
At 390 px a screen keeps every figure a decision needs and loses the ones that only support browsing. Receiving loses nothing at all, which is why it is the width the whole system was tested against first.
Nothing important is truncated
Product names, business names and quantities wrap rather than clip at every width. The only ellipsis in the system is on the invitation link, which is a token nobody reads by eye.

One contract, nine rows, four columns. Because the transformations are frozen at the element-class level, a screen built later inherits its small-screen behaviour instead of inventing it — and a reviewer can check any screen against this single table rather than against nine separate precedents.

1920 adds margin, not content
A sixth KPI, a wider table or a larger type scale would all be inventions of the extra room. The five KPI concepts are the five the data can justify, at every width.
The sidebar never changes shape
256 px, the same sixteen destinations in the same order, the same section labels. What varies between roles is which items exist; what varies between widths is only whether it is pinned or in a drawer.
Where the 1280 numbers come from
1280 − 256 sidebar − 48 padding = 976 px of table. Five KPI cards at 12 px gaps fit at 176 px each, which is why the value reads 691,700.00 with the currency moved to the sub-line rather than truncating.

The reference width is 1280, and 1920 is the same design with quieter edges. Drawing them together makes the restraint visible: the only difference a reviewer can find is the centred 1440 px content column and the KPI cards' extra breathing room.

Where 12 px is used, and where it is not
12 px carries hints, status pills and secondary metadata. No figure a person acts on — a balance, a quantity, a price, a minimum — is ever smaller than 13 px, and on the phone they are 15 to 20 px.
Why the desktop rows stay at 36 px
A 44 px row would cost eight of the twenty-five rows on a page, and a storekeeper scanning a full page with a mouse loses more from paging than from a smaller row. The 44 px rule is a touch rule, applied where there is touch.
The one place scrolling is allowed
Long ledgers scroll vertically, which is the direction a reader expects. No screen in the system requires a sideways drag to reach a figure or an action at any of the four widths.
Landscape and text scaling
At 844 × 390 the phone screens become the 768 layout, which is why that width exists in the contract. Doubling the reader's text size reflows every screen into a single column without clipping, because nothing is positioned by fixed height.

Reconciliation, closed. Nine transformations frozen, nine mobile-priority flows accounted for, and four production rules checked at all four widths — with the 390 px connected receipt in Gate 9 still the screen every other small-screen decision was tested against.
