Why five stages and not one form
Each stage asks one question whose answer changes the next. Collapsing them into a single screen would let someone map the wrong product to the wrong code with one careless save, and every future delivery would be wrong.
Look up is a button, not a keystroke
The same rule as finding a business: no live suggestions, no partial matches, no fuzzy correction. An exact code is either in their published catalog or it is not.
Every stage can be gone back to
Nothing is saved until stage five. Going back one step keeps what you already entered, and leaving the flow entirely creates nothing at all.

Two decisions, both deliberate. A supplier you are connected to right now, and a code you read off their paperwork — with the disabled connection visibly present and visibly refused rather than quietly missing from the list.

What Verified means, and does not
Verified by you, not by them
There is no approval step and no request to the supplier. Fresh Foods Ltd are not told this mapping exists, cannot see it, and cannot change it. It is your translation of their goods, and the word Verified refers to the person in your own workspace who confirmed it.
It unlocks exactly one thing
Receiving that item into stock in your own unit. It does not authorise an order, change a price, or grant either side any new visibility.
Stored with its evidence
The mapping keeps who confirmed it, when, the partner item as it read that day, and the factor. If the supplier renames the item later, the mapping still points at the same code and says the name changed.
One mapping, one direction
This does not create a matching mapping in their workspace, and it does not let them receive your goods in kilograms. Each side maps what it buys, for itself.
Who may create one
Owner, Admin and Purchasing Manager. An Inventory Manager receives against mappings but does not author them, and the New mapping action is absent for that role rather than disabled.

Create verified mapping, and the result is Verified. The action and the state use the same word, the confirmation is stored with a name and a date, and the success panel says what changed in operational terms rather than announcing a saved record.

Rejectedfrom Submitted only — they decline the order before it becomes theirs to fill
Cancelledfrom Draft, or from Submitted while they have not yet accepted — never once anything has shipped or been received
Two extra states, one reason. A private order has no counterparty to answer it, so it goes straight from Ordered to receiving. Here, Submitted and Accepted exist because a real business on the other side has to agree before you may expect anything.
Shipped is theirs to set. Only the supplier can say goods have left, and only you can say they arrived. Neither side can set the other's state, and neither side's stock is touched by the other's action.
Rejection is not failure. A rejected order stays in the list, readable, with the date. No reason is required, so none is displayed, and you may place another order immediately.

Six states, and every one of them owned by somebody. The buyer submits, cancels and receives; the supplier accepts, rejects and ships. No state can be set by the side it does not belong to, and the cancellation rule is the same physical rule as the private order: nothing shipped, nothing received.
