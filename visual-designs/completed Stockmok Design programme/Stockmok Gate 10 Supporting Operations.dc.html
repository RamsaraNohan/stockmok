No new component, no new capability
Every screen on this gate is assembled from the 25 frozen components. Nothing here writes anything the system cannot already write: a notification's read flag, a membership, an invitation, a settings value.

Refusals are screens, not redirects
A member who opens a page their role cannot see is told which role it needs and which one they hold. They are not bounced to the dashboard, because a silent redirect hides the mistake instead of naming it.

Only three things create one
1
A product crossed its minimum. Written only when the derived status actually changed, so a product that is already low does not notify again on every issue.
2
A connected order changed state. Accepted, rejected, shipped, received — the transitions the other business set, and yours.
3
A connection changed. A request arrived, was answered, or was disabled by either side.
What a notification is not
It is not an email — nothing is sent outside Stockmok in this release. It is not a task list: nothing here can be assigned, snoozed or escalated. And it carries no figure a partner should not see, because a notification about a connected order names the quantity in the unit that was ordered and nothing about your stock.
The only thing a reader may change
Read or unread. The title, the message and the time were written when the event happened and stay as they were, which is what makes the list usable as a record of what the business was told and when.

Status is never colour alone. Every row carries an icon, a worded state pill and the reading itself — 0.000 L against 40.000 L — so the difference between low and out survives greyscale, and the unread rows are marked by a dot and the word Unread as well as the tinted background.

Four rows, not eight
A Storekeeper is told about stock, because stock is what they act on. The order and connection notifications are not greyed out for them — they were never addressed to them, and the filter row shrinks to All and Unread accordingly.
Bounded by design
One event notifies at most fifty people, and the recipients are worked out before anything is written. A business that grows past that does not silently lose notifications: the limit is a stated rule rather than an accident of scale.

Three states of one screen, drawn rather than described. The empty state names what would appear here and offers a real next step; the skeleton holds the exact row geometry; and the role view differs by which events were ever addressed to the reader, not by disabling rows they can see.

One role per person
There is no role builder and no stacking of two roles. A person who needs both purchasing and stock work is made an Admin, and that decision is visible in one column of this table rather than buried in a permission matrix nobody reads.
Why the Owner row has words instead of buttons
A disabled Change role button invites somebody to keep clicking it. The destructive actions are absent from this row, and the reason is written where the buttons would have been.
Suspended, not deleted
Removing a member ends their access; it never removes their name from the movements, receipts and orders they recorded. A ledger that forgets who did something is worth less than no ledger at all.

Membership is the only thing that grants anything. The role in this table is what the server checks on every command — the sidebar column simply states how much of the product each role can see, so the difference between hiding an action and refusing it stays legible to whoever is administering the team.

Three kinds of setting
1
Changeable, and harmless
Name, industry, country, time zone, default store room, order prefix. Change them freely; nothing already recorded moves.
2
Changeable, and consequential
The two toggles. Each one states what it changes and, more importantly, what it does not: turning Network off hides three sidebar items, it does not end a relationship or touch an order.
3
Fixed, and shown anyway
Handle, currency, decimals. They are on the page because a person needs to read them, greyed with the word Fixed and a sentence saying why — never removed, which would look like a missing feature.

Recorded when it matters
A change to the two toggles or the default store room is written to the audit record with who made it and when. Renaming the business is not, because nothing about it can be disputed later.

An immutable field is a design statement, not an omission. The handle, the currency and the decimal precision are all drawn, all greyed, all labelled Fixed, and each one carries the single sentence that explains what would break if it could change.

Purchase Orders was never in this reader's sidebar. This screen appears because they followed a link or typed an address, and it says which role is needed and which one they hold.
Why not a redirect
Bouncing this person to the dashboard would leave them believing the link was broken and telling a colleague so. Naming the role turns a dead end into a request somebody can act on in thirty seconds.
Hiding is convenience, refusing is the boundary
The sidebar hides what a role cannot use. This screen exists because that hiding is not what stops anybody: the refusal is decided when the data is asked for, and it would happen even if every item were visible.

An archived record is not a missing page, so it never shows the not-found screen. Restoring a product is something the system genuinely does, which is why the button is here and not on the screen above.
No detective work
The not-found screen never guesses what you meant, and never says whether the thing at that address exists in some other business. Suggesting a near match would leak the existence of records the reader has no right to.
