The problem
Stock lives in a spreadsheet, and the spreadsheet lives with one person.
Counts drift between stores. Nobody can say what a supplier still owes. A purchase order is a WhatsApp message, and the only record of a receipt is the person who signed for it.
What Stockmok does
One record per business, with every change kept.
Products, warehouses and balances in one place. Every increase and decrease is written once and never edited afterwards, so today's figure can always be traced back to the movement that produced it.
What you get
Inventory control
Products with their own units, several warehouses, opening balances, adjustments with a reason, and a movement history that cannot be rewritten.
Purchasing and receiving
Raise an order against a supplier, receive it in parts, and see what is still outstanding. Your stock changes only when you record a receipt.
Connected businessesWhen enabled
Order from a supplier who also uses Stockmok. Their pack becomes your kilogram through a conversion you verify once. Neither side sees the other's stock or costs.
Reporting you can check
Stock on hand and purchase orders, filtered and exportable. Inventory value is stated as replacement cost, and the number always reconciles with the movements behind it.
Built for stock that is weighed, packed and moved
Stockmok fits businesses whose stock has real units and more than one store room.
HotelsRestaurants and kitchensFood distributionRetail with a back storeManufacturing supplies
Your books are yours
Each workspace is isolated. A member of one business cannot read another business's products, balances, costs or history — including a business you are connected to.
Roles decide what people can do
Owner, Admin, Inventory Manager, Procurement Manager, Storekeeper, Analyst and Viewer. The role you are given in a workspace decides what you can open and change.
Nothing is quietly overwritten
Stock changes are recorded as movements. Corrections are new entries with a reason attached, so the trail stays readable months later.
